I'm a Ph.D. student in Computer Science and Engineering at the [University of Michigan](https://cse.engin.umich.edu/). My research sits at the intersection of **machine learning and computer systems**, where I'm interested in building efficient infrastructure for large-scale AI. My work spans distributed training and inference, ML systems, performance optimization, and hardware-software co-design for modern machine learning workloads.

As an **SRC Research Scholar**, I work on hardware-software co-design for distributed LLM training and inference, with a focus on reducing communication overhead and improving throughput. My recent research explores bandwidth-efficient activation compression for pipeline-parallel LLMs and systems that coordinate heterogeneous compute resources across distributed environments.

Most recently, I was a **Research Scientist/Engineer Intern at Adobe**, where I expanded *Snoopy*, Adobe's distributed performance profiler, into a heterogeneous CPU-GPU profiling framework. My work included integrating NVIDIA CUPTI for unified CPU-GPU tracing, designing accurate cross-device time synchronization, extending support for Python, PyTorch, vLLM, and multi-GPU workloads, and developing validation and benchmarking tools for modern AI systems.

Before Michigan, I was an undergraduate researcher in Purdue's [NextGArch Lab](https://nextgarch.ecn.purdue.edu/), where I built and evaluated machine learning models for line-rate network traffic classification on programmable (PISA) switches. Through Purdue's SURF program, I also explored using LLMs to generate verifiable specifications for static verification with VeriFast and worked on deep learning methods for molecular structure prediction.

I received my B.S. in Computer Science from Purdue University, with minors in Mathematics, Accounting, and Management. I'm grateful to have been supported by an **SRC Research Scholarship**, the **Rackham First-Year CSE Ph.D. Fellowship**, and the **NSDI '25** and **NSDI '26 Student Grants**.

Outside of research, I enjoy mentoring undergraduate researchers and talking with prospective students about research, graduate school, and life at Michigan.
